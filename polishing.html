Full workflow

Wash

Two-bucket method — one bucket of soap solution, one of clean rinse water, both with a grit guard, so grit dragged off the mitt settles out instead of going back onto the car. pH-neutral soap only; anything stripping won't leave wax/sealant intact for the steps ahead.

Decontaminate

Iron remover first — dissolves embedded brake dust/rail dust chemically (look for the purple "bleeding" reaction, that's it working). Clay bar or clay mitt second, with a dedicated lubricant, to shear off anything bonded to the surface that iron remover can't touch (tree sap, overspray, mineral deposits).

The two-stage pass

How the cut and polish steps relate — same panel section, two different machine settings.

IPA wipe-down

10–20% isopropyl alcohol diluted with distilled water, or a premade panel wipe — strips oily compound/polish residue so you can judge the actual paint result, and so any wax/sealant/coating applied next bonds to bare paint instead of leftover product film.

  • GtechniqPanel Wipe (PWv2)ready-to-use, safe on softer OEM clears4.7(1,208 Amazon)
  • CarProEraserpanel prep before ceramic coating application4.7(737 Amazon)
  • DIY70% isopropyl alcohol : distilled water, mixed 1:1 to ~35% — works in most cases

Seal / wax / coat

Locks in the correction. Pick one tier: a wax/sealant hybrid for months of protection and low effort, or a ceramic coating for multi-year durability with a stricter application window.

Working a section

  1. 01
    Apply to the pad, not the panel — 3–5 pea-sized drops. Priming with a mist of detail spray helps the pad grip product.
  2. 02
    Spread at low speed (dial 1–2) across a 2×2 ft section before ramping up.
  3. 03
    Work at target speed — crosshatch passes, horizontal then vertical, ~50% overlap.
  4. 04
    Stop when the product turns clear on the paint — that's full breakdown, not before.
  5. 05
    Wipe and inspect with an IPA solution (10–20% isopropyl alcohol / distilled water) before judging the result.
  6. 06
    Repeat with the polish step — same crosshatch pattern, lower speed, light-to-no pressure.

Cleaning pads & microfibers

Between sections

Brush the pad face while it's still on the machine, low speed, to knock off caked/dried product before it builds up and starts reducing cut. Do this every section, not just at the end of the job.

  • Chemical GuysFoam Pad Cleaning Brushworks on foam and wool alike4.6(453 Amazon)

Pads, after the job

Rinse under warm water first to break loose bulk residue, then hand-wash — submerge in warm water with a mild dish soap or dedicated pad cleaner and repeatedly squeeze the soapy water through the pad face until it runs clear. Rinse thoroughly and squeeze out excess water; never wring or twist foam. Air-dry fully before storing — a damp pad left sealed up will mildew. Keep cutting-pad and polishing-pad washes separate so leftover compound doesn't contaminate a finishing pad.

Microfiber towels

Wash microfiber separately from every other fabric — cotton lint embeds in the fibers and ruins their scratch-free surface. Cold or cool water only; heat above ~105°F starts to melt and close the fibers' wedge shape, which is what makes them absorbent and non-scratching in the first place. Use a mild, additive-free detergent — no fragrance, no fabric softener, both coat the fibers and kill their cleaning ability. Separate paint towels from wax/sealant towels and glass towels; cross-contamination shows up as smearing or swirls on the next job. Dry on no-heat/air-fluff, or just hang dry — a hot dryer cycle does the same fiber damage as hot wash water.
