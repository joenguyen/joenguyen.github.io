Glass is not paint — different abrasive (cerium oxide, not paint compound), different pad (felt/wool/rayon, not foam), and a hard rule paint correction doesn't have: keep it wet or risk cracking the glass from heat. Keep the glass wet, always. Cerium oxide only cuts as a wet slurry — a dry pad on glass builds heat fast enough to thermally crack the pane, especially tempered side/rear glass. Mist water onto the work area every 15–30 seconds. If the slurry looks dry or is dragging, it has stopped working and started generating pure heat — stop and re-wet before continuing.

Tape off

Trim, cowl panel, A-pillars, wiper arms — anywhere a felt pad edge running at speed can catch and mar paint or plastic. Glass pads are more aggressive against soft trim than foam paint pads are.

Pre-treat water spots

If the marks are mineral deposits (hard water spots) rather than etching, a chemical remover often clears them without any abrasive polishing at all — try this before reaching for cerium oxide. Acid-based; never spray directly and let dwell, work a small area and wipe immediately. Skip this step and go straight to polishing if the marks are scratches/wiper haze rather than water spots.

Polish (remaining etching / wiper haze)

Felt, wool, or rayon pad + cerium oxide compound, dial 4–5, kept wet the entire time. Mix cerium oxide powder with water to a pancake-batter consistency if using a powder rather than a premixed compound. Work a 12"×12" area at a time, flat pad against the glass (never tilted — tilting concentrates pressure and risks edge burn), slow overlapping passes, re-wetting every 15–30 seconds.

Inspect

Clean the polished area, let it dry, and check under direct sun or a strong flashlight at a low angle — cerium oxide slurry residue can hide remaining light etching until it's fully wiped away. Streak-free glass cleaner again before final judgment.

Working a section

  1. 01
    Mix or prime — cerium oxide powder mixed with water to a pancake-batter consistency, or premixed compound applied straight to the pad.
  2. 02
    Divide the glass into quarters (or 12"×12" sections on a smaller pane) — work one section fully before moving on.
  3. 03
    Set dial 4–5, hold the pad flat against the glass — never tilted, which concentrates pressure at one edge.
  4. 04
    Slow overlapping passes, ~50% overlap, firm steady pressure — glass takes longer per section than paint, budget for it.
  5. 05
    Re-wet every 15–30 seconds with a spray bottle. A dry, dragging pad means it's stopped cutting and started generating heat — the exact condition that risks cracking.
  6. 06
    Wipe and inspect with streak-free glass cleaner before deciding a section is done — slurry film hides remaining defects.

Cleaning pads & microfibers

Felt / wool / rayon pads, after the job

Rinse thoroughly under running water immediately after use — dried cerium oxide slurry cakes into the fibers and is far harder to remove once set. Hand-wash with mild soap, working the water through the pad face, then rinse until it runs clear. Air-dry fully before storing; never wring wool or felt, squeeze gently instead. Keep glass pads stored separately from paint pads — cerium oxide is a harder abrasive than most paint polishes and will mar clear coat if cross-contaminated onto a paint pad.

Microfiber towels

Same rules as paint towels: wash microfiber separately from other fabrics, cold or cool water only (heat above ~105°F melts and closes the fibers), mild additive-free detergent, no fabric softener, air-dry or no-heat dryer setting. Keep towels used for cerium oxide slurry separate from glass-cleaner/streak-free towels — leftover abrasive residue on a "clean" towel will haze the next wipe-down.
